# Audience and market

Who we are building for, and what they will pay. This is the source for every audience claim in the scope. Compiled 2026.

Marked **unverified** where a claim could not be confirmed against a primary source. Do not treat an unverified figure as fact in a spec.

---

## 1. Institutions and programmes

**Public universities.** MINESUP counts 8 historically and 11 currently. Eight five percent of enrolment is in comprehensive universities and eight five percent in public providers. Enrolment grew from 77,707 in 2002 to 323,672 in 2018.

- Francophone state universities: Yaoundé I, Yaoundé II, Douala, Dschang, Ngaoundéré, Maroua
- Anglophone state universities: **Buea and Bamenda only**
- Garoua, Bertoua, Ebolowa and Foumban are mostly auxiliary campuses of Université de Yaoundé I, not separate universities

Tertiary gross enrolment is around 16.7 percent. The system is massified without capacity behind it.

**Why Buea and Bamenda first.** They are the only two English speaking state universities and they serve two English speaking regions. Anglophone competition is structurally tighter, so this is the most motivated and least served cohort in the country. UNCDF research specifically identifies the English speaking minority as early adopters of technology.

**Private universities.** Numerous and fragmented. Catholic University of Bamenda, ICT University, Fotso Victor, Université des Montagnes, Université Saint Jérôme de Douala. Smaller classes, heavy reliance on part time lecturers.

**Polytechnics and grandes écoles**, around 46 under state universities and technical ministries. École Polytechnique de Yaoundé, Douala and Bafoussam, ENSPT, ENSAI Ngaoundéré. Public works runs through ENTP and the IUT civil engineering tracks.

**IUT** (Instituts Universitaires de Technologie). Douala, Bandjoun, Ngaoundéré. Offer DUT, BTS and Licence de Technologie. Fees 350,000 to 500,000 XAF for a Licence. Students pay in two instalments of half, which is worth copying for our billing norm.

**BTS.** Run centrally by MINESUP at four main centres. Fees 35,500 to 55,500 XAF by specialty category, separate from school fees. Taught in French.

**HND.** Two years, four semesters, taught in English, run almost entirely by private institutions. Widely sought as a cheaper route to a degree. We already model this tier.

**Teacher training.** ENS at Yaoundé I, HTTTC at Buea and Kumba.

**Unverified:** ENET as a distinct current entity, ENGC, ENSAM, specific scholarship programmes beyond those listed below.

## 2. Who the student is

Best dataset: University of Yaoundé I, n=1,200, May to July 2024.

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Female | 52.5 percent |
| Under 24 | 89 percent |
| Undergraduate | 98 percent |
| Live off campus | 97.8 percent (74.5 percent with family) |
| Supported by parents | 92 percent |
| **Say parental support is insufficient** | **48 percent** |
| Hold a part time job | 26 percent |

Monthly income received from a third party: 14.6 percent receive 15,000 XAF or less, 25.9 percent receive 15,001 to 25,000, 10.5 percent 25,001 to 35,000, 11.9 percent 35,001 to 50,000. **The modal student lives on 15,000 to 25,000 XAF a month.**

Two facts that bound everything we design:

- Public undergraduate registration is a flat 50,000 XAF a year, which is about 4,167 XAF a month. Tuition is deliberately near symbolic. A real family pays far more for a private degree: Biaka University Institute of Buea lists 325,000 to 475,000 XAF a year.
- Only 41.6 percent of Cameroonians aged 15 and over used a smartphone in 2024. We are targeting the top percentile of the country on device quality.

**Working and academic risk.** Working raises repeat year probability by 4.8 percentage points, and the harmful threshold is above thirty hours a week.

**Funding.** Scholarships reach about 6.7 percent of students. The MTN Cameroon YaMo bourse at 2,000,000 XAF a year is the largest single one.

## 3. What studying actually looks like

- **Data costs.** 1.63 US dollars per GB against 0.38 in Malawi and 0.55 in Rwanda. A 5 GB bundle costs 5.72 percent of GNI per capita against a 2 percent affordability threshold. Measured student spend: 70.3 percent spent under 1,000 XAF a month before distance learning, 75.5 percent spent up to 2,999 XAF during it. **Budget against a 1,000 to 3,000 XAF monthly ceiling.**
- **Hardware.** Over ninety percent of students have no laptop. The state programme's own document says this. Target device is roughly 2 GB RAM and 32 GB storage.
- **Power.** Irregular grid supply is a named barrier to ICT use, worse on remote campuses. Nothing long running or video first.
- **Overcrowding.** One lecturer addressing up to a thousand students. The teacher to student ratio went from 1 in 25 to 1 in 54. Practical sessions are frequently skipped, so **self study is the de facto mode of study.**
- **Libraries.** Weak, print only, and students must buy their own data to reach the internet on campus.
- **Materials.** Past papers circulate as photocopies and are hard to find. This is why a student photographed their own notes is our primary content source, not a gap to be filled by an admin library.
- **Language.** Two separate systems, not bilingual institutions. English in the Northwest and Southwest, French everywhere else. Student writing code mixes English to French, then Pidgin, then home languages.
- **Integrity.** 98.3 percent of a 1,525 person sample across 18 institutions had cheated at some point and 93 percent at university. Seventy percent of fraud cases at one university involved phones. 81 percent of students at one teacher training college admitted internet plagiarism. Motive is documented as "you must pass". **The AI must read as a tutor and grader, never as an answer machine.**

## 4. What they use today

WhatsApp is the classroom. It was chosen over Zoom and Google Classroom specifically because it does not consume much data. During distance teaching one university's academic share of WhatsApp time went from 22.3 percent to 85.6 percent. A 370 student survey elsewhere found the same apps primarily distracting.

| Product | Focus | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| VALIDE | Yaoundé I and II past papers, AI tutor, offline | **100 percent free**, 25,000 registered claimed, Cameroon AI Impact Award |
| eStudyUniverse | GCE plus concours, AI tutor, offline | Free core, credit top ups, 7,000 person WhatsApp channel |
| GCE Study App | Secondary | Buea based, MTN and Orange Money, tiered |
| HEQA Solutions | Student app | Bilingual, offline, leaderboard, mobile money |
| Cameroon Past Questions | PDF library | One time lifetime purchase |
| Kamerpower | PDF packs | Sells files, app is free |
| A Level Cameroon | Secondary | Free with ads, top complaint is too many ads |
| SkyDew | AI over SMS and USSD | Born from the 2017 Anglophone internet shutdown |

**The gap.** Nobody has solved higher education well. VALIDE covers only two universities and is free with no monetisation. The secondary school apps cover the wrong level. Nobody has a credible university product for the Anglophone state universities sold by mobile money.

## 5. What they will pay, and how much

**No Cameroonian consumer platform publishes pricing.** The clearest analysis found states this explicitly and calls its own figures illustrative for lack of a verified public price grid.

Observed consumer price points:

| Product | Audience | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Cameroon Past Questions | Students | 500 XAF lifetime, unlimited downloads |
| HEQA Solutions | Students | 399 XAF a week, or 1,099 XAF a month |
| GCE Study App | Students | 1,100 XAF a month, 3,100 XAF a month |
| SmartED Africa | Secondary | 1,600 XAF a month, 4,600 XAF a year |
| 10sur10 | Secondary | 5,000 XAF a month |
| Azou | Families | 60,000 XAF a year individual, or 20,000 to 30,000 in instalments |
| MySmartAcademy | Secondary | 29,900 to 39,000 XAF a year |
| Private human tutor | Students | 20,000 to 35,000 XAF a month per subject |
| Kamerpower PDF pack | Students | 18,500 to 19,500 XAF |
| Biaka University Buea | Students | 325,000 to 475,000 XAF a year for a degree |

**Reading these numbers.** The proven consumer band is 1,600 to 5,000 XAF a month. Everything above that is a school budget, not a student budget. Anchor the price against the 50,000 XAF registration: at or below its monthly equivalent a student sees it as free, above it the student needs a justification they control.

The Azou split payment pattern, full price or two instalments or three, instalments discounted, is the market native normalisation move for a September budget with no cash. IUT fees already split fifty fifty across two tranches, so per semester is the norm the market already thinks in.

**The counter evidence, and it is serious.** African consumers generally do not have appetite to pay for supplementary education because there is no disposable income for it. Forty percent of Cameroonians live below the poverty line and even 7,500 XAF a year of primary fees is an obstacle. On the other hand, price level is not the barrier to adoption, value delivered is, and tertiary education is the single strongest predictor of mobile money adoption in the country. **Mobile money is proven. Willingness to pay for a study tool is the constraint.**

**Payment behaviour.** MTN Mobile Money holds about 60 percent of the mobile money share and Orange the rest, and students often carry both. In Buea, students drive about 60 percent of transaction volume and lean MTN, with Orange perceived as a French product. **Do not ship an Orange first or French only flow.** Merchant payments cost 1 to 2 percent of value. Daily mobile money limits are typically 500,000 to 1,000,000 XAF.

**Verdict.** Sell per semester, not per month. The competitor analysis is explicit that a paid platform at 7,000 XAF a month loses money at 120 subscribers.

## 6. The academic calendar, and when to sell

State universities do **not** follow the Anglophone September to June pattern. Lectures begin in October.

| Window | Event | What it means commercially |
|---|---|---|
| September | Registration fees collected, 50,000 XAF | Money leaves the family. **Do not sell** |
| October | Lectures begin, semester one | New semester intention |
| December | Semester one exams | Value window one |
| January to February | Holiday, results, semester two registration | Fee drain resumes |
| February to April | Semester two teaching | |
| May to June | Semester two exams, the bigger block | Value window two, the biggest |
| June to July | Resits, convocation | Results land, either it worked or the next problem appears |
| July to September | Long vacation | Dead zone |

**Best moments to sell:** the last three weeks before semester one exams, mid April to end of May, and June or July when results land. **Worst:** September and October, and July and August.

## 7. The recommendation that follows from all of this

**Sell a semester pass, not a subscription.** A tiered pack scoped to one semester, sold as one payment, with an internal metered AI allowance. Credits are the escape valve, not the headline. Round numbers only, because mobile money reconciliation has to be trivial.

**Free forever, capped on AI.** Not a trial. A trial imposes a clock on a student whose exam timetable we do not control, and cutting a student off in the week of exams is a permanent loss plus a public complaint. The free tier gets the full archive and unlimited viewing, a real but limited number of AI questions, readiness, streaks and leaderboards, and one timed mock per semester. The scarcity is in the AI, not in the archive.

**A hardship route, named openly.** Nearly half of students say parental support is insufficient, so some will never pay. Ambassadors who refer enough students earn a free pass, which costs nothing and pays in acquisition. Never gate the archive, because gating it makes us a competitor to free PDFs instead of a complement to them.

**Be careful about the arithmetic.** Heavy AI use costs about 130 XAF a month on a cheap model and about 200 with provider fees, against a 2,500 XAF pass. A month of AI use is roughly 25 times what a student spends on all their internet. Prompt caching on a stable system prefix cuts that by three to four times.
