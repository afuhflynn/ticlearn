## The questions parentsask first.

- What subjects does it cover?





Every subject in grades 7-12. If you add a weekly tutor, they’re matched to the subject and class that needs it.

- Will it just do the work for them?





No, and the way it avoids that is the interesting part. Brainly reads each moment and picks one of three responses: a full explanation when your teen is genuinely missing something, a nudge when they’re close and just aren’t connecting it, and a question back when the signals say they’re trying to hand the work over. That decision is made per moment rather than per student, because the same teen needs instruction on Tuesday and pushing on Thursday. In Worksheets, they also can’t skip ahead; the next step stays shut until the current one is right.

- How is this different from ChatGPT?





A general chatbot answers the question your teen typed and forgets it happened. Brainly works backwards from the question to what’s actually missing underneath it; two students can ask the same thing word for word and need completely different help. It also explains using the method their class is using, not whichever approach is most elegant, because an answer in different notation than their teacher used isn’t help at ten at night. And it keeps what it learns: October’s gap is what November’s test prep drills.

- Is it safe for my teen?





Brainly is built for students. It stays inside schoolwork, it’s age-appropriate by design, and there’s no open-ended companion behavior. It explains without doing the work for them. Brainly is also a member of the EdSafe AI Alliance. For parents, your Learning Advisor can text you when something’s worth knowing, so you stay in the loop without having to check.

- What if it isn’t right for us?





First step is a call with your learning advisor. Most of the time it’s a fixable mismatch rather than a dead end, and they’ll usually spot it before you do. If it still isn’t landing, you have a **100% money-back guarantee** for your first 30 days, and you can cancel any time after that. Your plan simply runs to the end of the period you’ve paid for. We’d rather hear about it than not: if it isn’t holding up in your house, on a school night, that’s the thing we most want to know.
